About

My research focuses on learning with ordinal supervision, where labels carry an inherent order, as in ratings, severity grades, and quality scores. Standard objectives such as cross-entropy treat these labels as unordered categories, which can leave both learned representations and predictions misaligned with the structure of the label space. I develop training objectives and representation learning methods, with a particular focus on contrastive learning, that preserve this structure.

I am also interested in data-efficient learning, particularly training strategies for active learning, and in assessing the reliability of large language models as automated evaluators.

Research interests

  • Ordinal alignmentLosses and predictions that respect the order between classes, so a model treats a 4 as closer to a 5 than to a 1.
  • Representation learningHow embedding spaces take shape, with a focus on contrastive learning and whether the learned geometry reflects the structure of the labels.
  • LLMs as judgesHow reliably language models rate and rank outputs, and how their judgments line up with human ones.
  • Active learningChoosing what to label and how to train, so models improve with fewer annotations.
