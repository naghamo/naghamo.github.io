About

My research focuses on learning and evaluation with ordinal supervision, where labels carry an inherent order, as in ratings, severity grades, and quality scores. Standard methods often ignore this structure. I develop training objectives and representation learning methods, particularly for contrastive learning, that preserve it. I also design statistical tests for assessing whether large language models can serve as reliable judges and annotators.

I am also interested in data-efficient learning, particularly active learning.

Research interests

Ordinal alignment

Learning and evaluation for tasks where labels have a natural order.

Representation learning

Learning representations that capture the structure of the data and labels.

Large language models

Understanding how large language models behave and when they can be relied on in practice.

LLMs as judges

Assessing when large language models can be trusted to evaluate and rank model outputs.

LLMs as annotators

Using large language models to label data, and testing when their labels can stand in for human ones.

Active learning

Training effective models with limited labeled data.

Publications

Train Smarter, Not Harder: Switching Signal-Guided Training in Active Learning

Nagham Omar, Maya Rozenshtein, Evgeny Mishlyakov, Avigdor Gal

EMNLP 2026 Main Conference, poster in Budapest
Paper Code

In active learning, the model can either be retrained from scratch or fine-tuned from the previous checkpoint after each labeling round, yet this choice is rarely treated as a design decision. We show that it has exploitable structure: retraining pays off in early rounds, when each new batch can substantially shift the labeled distribution, while fine-tuning becomes the safer option once the model's trajectory stabilizes. We introduce HybridAL, an adaptive schedule that tracks an online stabilization signal and switches from retraining to fine-tuning once stabilization is sustained. We study two complementary signals, one based on changes in the weights' spectral exponent and one based on changes in validation accuracy, which offer different trade-offs between training time and calibration. Across three encoder backbones and six text classification tasks, HybridAL matches the final macro-F1 of both retraining and fine-tuning, cuts retraining time by up to 49%, and recovers much of the calibration advantage of retraining.
